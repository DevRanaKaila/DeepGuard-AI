Chapter 01
The Problem: Why Deepfakes Are Dangerous
AI can now create fake videos so real that even experts can't tell the difference
🎭 What is a Deepfake?
A deepfake is a fake video where someone's face is replaced with another person's face using AI. Think of it like a digital mask — but so realistic that you can't see the seams.

Tools like DeepFaceLab and FaceSwap use neural networks to learn how a person's face moves, then paste it perfectly onto another person's body in a video.
⚠️ Why is This Dangerous?
• Banking Fraud: Criminals create fake video calls impersonating bank executives to steal money
• Political Manipulation: Fake speeches of politicians spread during elections
• Identity Theft: Bypass face recognition in e-KYC verification
• Defamation: Create fake compromising videos of innocent people

$25 Billion lost to deepfake fraud globally (2024)
🧠 The Core Insight: Why Current Detectors Fail
❌ Current Approach (Spatial Only)

Most detectors look at the pixels of the image — trying to find blurry edges, weird skin textures, or mismatched lighting.

Problem: When videos are uploaded to YouTube, WhatsApp, or Instagram, the platform compresses the video. This compression destroys the subtle pixel clues, dropping accuracy from 98% → 65%.
✅ Our Approach (Spatial + Frequency)

We look at both the pixels AND the mathematical frequency patterns hidden inside the image.

Key Discovery: AI-generated faces leave invisible "fingerprints" in the frequency domain (like ripples in a pond) that survive even heavy compression. We exploit this!

Chapter 04
The Secret Weapon: 2D Discrete Cosine Transform
How we see what the human eye cannot — explained with a real-world analogy
🎵 The Music Analogy
Imagine you hear a song. Your ear perceives it as one smooth sound. But a music equalizer breaks it into bass, mid, and treble frequencies.

The DCT does the exact same thing to images!

It breaks a face image into its frequency components:
• Low frequencies = Smooth areas (skin, background)
• Mid frequencies = Edges and contours (nose, eyes, jaw)
• High frequencies = Fine details (hair strands, pores, textures)

GAN-generated faces are missing certain high frequencies — like a song played with the treble turned off. You might not hear/see it, but the math can detect it!
📐 The Mathematical Formula
The 2D Discrete Cosine Transform converts each pixel value f(x,y) into frequency coefficients F(u,v):
F(u,v) = α(u) · α(v) · Σ Σ f(x,y) · cos[π(2x+1)u / 2N] · cos[π(2y+1)v / 2N]
In plain English:
Each frequency coefficient F(u,v) tells us "how much of a particular wave pattern is present in the image."

The killer feature: After computing DCT, we calculate the Radial Average Spectrum:
A(r) = average of |F(u,v)| for all (u,v) at radius r = √(u² + v²)
Real faces have a smooth, natural decline in this spectrum.
Fake faces show suspicious bumps and periodic spikes!
👁️ What Does the DCT Spectrum Look Like?
Real Face DCT
Smooth, circular gradient. Energy concentrated in the center (low frequencies). Natural decay outward.
Fake Face DCT (Subtle)
Grid-like artifacts appear as periodic bright spots. These come from the GAN's upsampling layers.
Fake Face DCT (Obvious)
Strong cross-shaped or grid artifacts. Clear evidence of artificial upsampling in the generator network.
Chapter 05
Stream I: EfficientNet-B4 Spatial Backbone
The "eyes" of our system — 19.3 million neurons looking at pixel details
🏗️ What is EfficientNet?
EfficientNet is a family of neural networks designed by Google Brain.

The key innovation: Instead of making the network just deeper or wider, EfficientNet scales all three dimensions simultaneously using a mathematical formula:

• Depth (more layers) × 1.8
• Width (more neurons per layer) × 1.4
• Resolution (bigger input images) × 1.0

B4 variant = Sweet spot of accuracy vs. speed

It was pre-trained on 14 million images (ImageNet), so it already knows what natural objects, textures, and faces look like. We fine-tune it to spot the unnatural patterns in deepfakes.
🔧 Internal Building Blocks
EfficientNet uses special modules called MBConv (Mobile Inverted Bottleneck Convolution):

Input (256×256×3)
  ↓ MBConv × 6 stages
  ↓ Each stage has:
    • Expansion (1×1 conv, expand channels)
    • Depthwise (3×3 conv, learn patterns)
    • Squeeze-Excitation (attention!)
    • Projection (1×1 conv, compress)
  ↓ Global Average Pooling
Output: 1,792-dimensional vector

Squeeze-and-Excitation (SE) is like an attention mechanism: it learns which channels (features) are most important and amplifies them while suppressing noise.

Chapter 06
Attention-Gated Fusion & Classification
How two AI brains merge their opinions into one confident verdict
🔗 The Fusion Formula (Simplified)
Think of it like this: You ask two experts to examine a painting.

• Expert A (Spatial): "I see some blurry edges. 60% suspicious."
• Expert B (Frequency): "I see strong GAN grid artifacts. 95% suspicious."

The Attention Gate is a third AI that decides: "Expert B is more confident this time, so I'll weight their opinion more heavily."
g = sigmoid(W × [spatial_features ∥ freq_features] + b)

fused = [g ⊙ spatial] ∥ [(1-g) ⊙ freq]

g is a learned gate (0 to 1) that dynamically balances both streams
⊙ = element-wise multiplication, ∥ = concatenation
🧠 Classification Head
After fusion, the 1024-d vector goes through the final decision layers:
Fused Vector (1024-d)
  ↓ Fully Connected Layer (1024 → 256)
  ↓ Layer Normalization
  ↓ ReLU Activation
  ↓ Dropout (40% neurons randomly turned off = prevents overfitting)
  ↓ Fully Connected Layer (256 → 1)
  ↓ Sigmoid Activation (squashes output to 0–1)
Output: 0.0 = 100% Real ←→ 1.0 = 100% Fake
📉 Loss Function: Binary Cross-Entropy with Label Smoothing
L = -(1/M) Σ [ y_smooth · log(ŷ) + (1 - y_smooth) · log(1 - ŷ) ]

where y_smooth = y × (1 - ε) + 0.5 × ε,    ε = 0.1
Why Label Smoothing? Instead of telling the model "this is 100% fake", we say "this is 95% fake." This prevents the model from becoming over-confident, which helps it generalize to new types of deepfakes it hasn't seen before.

📐 Evaluation Metrics Explained
AUC-ROC (0.968): How well the model separates real from fake, regardless of threshold. 1.0 = perfect.

F1-Score: Balanced measure of precision (how many flagged fakes are actually fake) and recall (how many fakes were caught).

EER (Equal Error Rate): The point where false accepts = false rejects. Lower is better. Used in biometric security.

Inference Speed (<50ms): Fast enough for real-time video call screening.
