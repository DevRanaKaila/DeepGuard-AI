02. What is a Deepfake?

Simple Analogy

Imagine you have a rubber mask of Elon Musk's face. You wear it, and from a distance, people think you're Elon Musk. A deepfake is like that — but done with AI at the pixel level. The AI takes your face in a video and replaces it with someone else's face, frame by frame, so smoothly that it's nearly impossible to detect with the human eye.

How are Deepfakes Made? (Technical Version)

  • Step 1 — Encoder: A neural network learns to extract the "identity" features from a face (who the person is) and "expression" features (what emotion they're showing).
  • Step 2 — Decoder: A separate neural network takes those features and reconstructs a new face. By swapping encoders between person A and person B, you get person A's expression on person B's face.
  • Step 3 — GAN Training: A "discriminator" network judges whether the output looks real. The generator and discriminator train against each other until the fake looks indistinguishable.
  • The Flaw: The decoder uses "transposed convolutions" for upsampling (making the image bigger). This process leaves periodic mathematical "ripples" in the frequency domain — invisible to eyes, but detectable by DCT analysis.

03. Why Existing Systems Fail

❌ The Compression Problem

When you upload a video to WhatsApp, YouTube, or Instagram, the platform compresses it using H.264/H.265 codecs to save storage. This compression:

  • Removes subtle pixel-level manipulation artifacts (the clues spatial detectors rely on)
  • Causes spatial-only models like XceptionNet to drop from 98.5% accuracy → 89.3% (c23 compression) → much lower on heavy compression
  • Means deepfakes shared on social media survive detection by most systems

Why DCT fingerprints survive compression: The periodic GAN upsampling artifacts appear at specific frequencies in the DCT spectrum. JPEG/H.264 compression preserves low-to-mid frequency energy while discarding only extreme high frequencies. The GAN fingerprints sit in mid-frequencies — they survive compression.

05. EfficientNet-B4 Explained Simply

The Analogy: A Smart Camera

Think of EfficientNet-B4 as a camera that automatically adjusts its zoom, resolution, and depth-of-field simultaneously for maximum detail. Normal cameras (networks) adjust only one thing at a time. EfficientNet adjusts all three using a mathematical formula called compound scaling.

How It Works (Layer by Layer)

  1. Input: 256×256 RGB face image (3 color channels)
  2. Stage 1–2 (Shallow layers): Detects simple edges, corners, color boundaries — "Is there an edge here?"
  3. Stage 3–4 (Mid layers): Recognizes facial parts — eyes, nose, mouth, jaw shape
  4. Stage 5–6 (Deep layers): Detects abstract features — skin texture smoothness, blending artifacts, reflection consistency
  5. Global Average Pool → 1792-d vector: Compresses all that spatial knowledge into 1,792 numbers describing the face
Input (256×256×3) → MBConv Blocks × 32 → GlobalAvgPool → Output: [batch, 1792] MBConv = Mobile Inverted Bottleneck Convolution → Expand channels (1×1 conv) → Apply depthwise 3×3 conv (learn patterns) → Squeeze-Excite attention (focus on important channels) → Project back down (1×1 conv)

Why B4 and not B7? B7 has 66M parameters (254 MB). B4 has 19.3M parameters (~85 MB) and achieves 97%+ of B7's accuracy. B4 is 3× smaller and 4× faster — perfect for a student project running on CPU.

06. DCT Explained Simply — The Music Analogy

🎵 Imagine a Song

When you listen to music, you hear it as one combined sound. But a music equalizer breaks it down into bass (low frequencies), mid-range, and treble (high frequencies). You can see visually if the bass is too loud or the treble is missing.

DCT does the exact same thing to images. It breaks a 256×256 face image into frequency components — low (smooth backgrounds), medium (facial edges), and high (skin texture, fine detail).

F(u,v) = α(u)·α(v) · Σ Σ f(x,y) · cos[π(2x+1)u/2N] · cos[π(2y+1)v/2N] What this means: f(x,y) = pixel value at position (x,y) in the image F(u,v) = how much of frequency pattern (u,v) exists in the image u,v = frequency coordinates (u=0,v=0 is the DC component = average brightness) N = 256 (image size) α(k) = normalization factor: √(1/N) if k=0, else √(2/N)

What the DCT Reveals About Fakes

  • Real face DCT: Energy concentrated in center (low frequencies). Smooth radial decline outward. Natural.
  • Fake face DCT: Periodic bright spots appear in a grid pattern. These are from the GAN generator's transposed convolution (upsampling) layers creating regular repetitions.
  • Radial spectrum A(r): A graph where x-axis = frequency radius, y-axis = average energy. Real faces show smooth decline. Fake faces show bumps at specific radii.
📢 Viva tip: If professor asks "explain DCT", say: "Sir, DCT converts a spatial image into frequency components, similar to how a music equalizer separates a song into bass, mid, and treble. GAN generators leave systematic periodic artifacts in the DCT spectrum because their transposed convolution upsampling layers create repeating patterns — like a printer that always has a slightly uneven ink head."

07. Attention-Gated Fusion — The Jury Analogy

⚖️ Imagine Two Expert Witnesses

You're a judge. Expert A (Spatial) says: "I see slight blurring near the jaw — 60% suspicious." Expert B (Frequency) says: "I see a strong GAN grid pattern — 95% suspicious." The attention gate is your brain: it decides to trust Expert B more this time because their evidence is stronger.

Now imagine the video is heavily compressed. Expert A (Spatial) can barely see anything through the compression noise — their evidence is weak. Expert B (Frequency) can still see the GAN fingerprints because they survive compression. The gate automatically shifts trust to Expert B.

Step 1: Concatenate features combined = [v_spatial (1792-d) || v_freq (512-d)] → 2304-d vector Step 2: Compute gate g = sigmoid(W_gate × combined + b_gate) g is a vector of 1792 values, each between 0 and 1 Step 3: Apply gate weighted_spatial = g × v_spatial (element-wise) weighted_freq = (1-g) × W_proj × v_freq v_fused = concat(weighted_spatial, weighted_freq) → 3584-d → FC → 1024-d

The W_gate matrix is learned during training. The model sees thousands of (fake, real) × (compressed, uncompressed) examples and learns: "when spatial features are uncertain, trust frequency more."

08. Grad-CAM Explained Simply — The Spotlight

🔦 Imagine a Spotlight

After the model says "FAKE," you ask it: "Show me what you were looking at when you made that decision." Grad-CAM works backwards through the network — it finds which parts of the last convolutional layer's feature maps were most responsible for the FAKE decision, then projects those locations back onto the original image as a colored heatmap.

How Grad-CAM Works (Steps)

  1. Forward pass: Run the image through EfficientNet-B4. Get the FAKE prediction.
  2. Backward pass: Compute gradients of the FAKE score with respect to the last conv layer's feature maps.
  3. Weight maps: Average the gradients over all positions → get importance weights for each feature map channel.
  4. Weighted sum: Multiply each feature map by its weight and sum → get a rough activation map.
  5. Upsample + colorize: Resize back to 256×256, apply red-yellow-blue colormap (red = most suspicious).
📢 Viva tip: "Sir, Grad-CAM applies gradient backpropagation to identify which spatial regions of the input image contributed most to the deepfake classification decision. Red regions on the heatmap indicate high importance — these are the areas where the model detected manipulation artifacts, such as blending boundaries or unnatural skin texture transitions."

09. What Each File in Your Project Does

src/models/spatial_stream.py
Loads EfficientNet-B4 from the timm library (pretrained on ImageNet). Removes the final classification head. Returns 1792-dimensional feature vectors for each input face image.
src/data/dct_transform.py
Converts RGB face images to grayscale, applies 2D DCT using scipy, computes log-scaled magnitude spectrum, and calculates the radial azimuthal average A(r). Returns a 256×256 frequency map tensor.
src/models/frequency_stream.py
A 4-stage Spectral CNN that processes the DCT frequency map. Uses depthwise separable convolutions (faster, fewer parameters). Outputs a 512-dimensional frequency feature vector.
src/models/fusion.py
The attention-gated bilinear fusion module. Takes the 1792-d spatial and 512-d frequency vectors, projects them to the same space, computes the gate g, and outputs a 1024-d fused representation.
src/models/dual_stream_net.py
The main model class that combines SpatialStream + FrequencyStream + AttentionFusion + Classification Head. Input: (x_rgb, x_dct). Output: dictionary with logits, probabilities, and intermediate features.
src/models/selim_classifier.py
Implements Selim Seferbekov's Kaggle DFDC 1st-place model architecture (EfficientNet-B7, spatial only). Used to load the pretrained 254MB checkpoint for inference comparison. Shows what a spatial-only detector looks like.
src/utils/gradcam.py
GradCAM class that hooks into the EfficientNet's last convolutional layer. Computes gradient-weighted activation maps and returns them as a colored heatmap overlay on the original face image.
train.py
Complete training pipeline. Uses AdamW optimizer, CosineAnnealing learning rate schedule, early stopping (patience=6), mixed precision (AMP for GPU), and BCE with label smoothing (ε=0.1).
inference.py
Command-line inference script. Auto-detects whether selim_dfdc_b7.pth or best_model.pth is available. Handles image + video inference. Outputs Real/Fake verdict with confidence percentage.
app.py
The Streamlit web dashboard. Has 4 pages: Home (upload + analyze), How It Works (explainer), Demo (quick test), and About. Shows Plotly confidence gauge, frame timeline, DCT spectrum, and Grad-CAM heatmap.

11. 25 Expected Viva Questions + Answers

Q1. What is the objective of your project?
"Sir, the objective is to develop a dual-stream deep learning system that detects deepfake videos by simultaneously analyzing facial images in the spatial pixel domain using EfficientNet-B4 and the frequency domain using 2D Discrete Cosine Transform. The system addresses the key limitation of existing detectors — their accuracy degradation under video compression — and provides Grad-CAM visual explanations and an interactive web dashboard."
Q2. What is a deepfake?
"Sir, a deepfake is an AI-generated video in which a person's face is replaced with another person's face using deep learning models — specifically Generative Adversarial Networks. The GAN learns to generate realistic facial appearances by training a generator against a discriminator until the fake is indistinguishable from real footage."
Q3. What is EfficientNet and why did you choose B4?
"Sir, EfficientNet is a family of CNNs designed by Google Brain that achieves superior accuracy by compound-scaling depth, width, and resolution simultaneously using the formula d=α^φ, w=β^φ, r=γ^φ. I chose B4 over B7 because B4 has 19.3 million parameters versus 66 million for B7, making it 3× more efficient while achieving comparable accuracy for deepfake feature extraction. B4 is also more suitable for CPU deployment."
Q4. What is DCT and how does it help detect deepfakes?
"Sir, the 2D Discrete Cosine Transform converts a spatial image into its frequency components — similar to a music equalizer that breaks a song into bass, mid, and treble. GAN generators use transposed convolutions for upsampling, which introduce periodic checkerboard artifacts in the frequency domain. These artifacts appear as regular spikes in the DCT spectrum and persist even after video compression, making them reliable fingerprints for detection."
Q5. What is attention-gated fusion?
"Sir, attention-gated fusion is a learnable mechanism that computes a gate vector g = sigmoid(W × [v_spatial || v_freq] + b). This gate assigns a weight between 0 and 1 to each feature dimension, dynamically controlling how much each stream contributes. Under heavy video compression, the spatial stream becomes less reliable, so the gate automatically shifts trust toward the frequency stream."
Q6. What is Grad-CAM?
"Sir, Grad-CAM stands for Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping. It computes the gradient of the classification score with respect to the last convolutional layer's feature maps, averages the gradients channel-wise to get importance weights, then creates a weighted sum of the feature maps. This produces a heatmap showing which facial regions most influenced the deepfake detection decision."
Q7. What datasets did you use?
"Sir, I used FaceForensics++ (FF++), which contains 1,000 real YouTube videos and 4,000 manipulated videos created using four methods: Deepfakes, Face2Face, FaceSwap, and NeuralTextures. It has three compression tiers: raw, c23 (light), and c40 (heavy). I also used Celeb-DF v2 for cross-dataset evaluation, which contains 590 real and 5,639 high-quality celebrity deepfake videos."
Q8. What is label smoothing and why did you use it?
"Sir, label smoothing replaces hard labels (0 or 1) with soft labels (0.05 and 0.95) by the formula y_smooth = y(1-ε) + 0.5ε where ε=0.1. This prevents the model from becoming overconfident and improves generalization to unseen deepfake methods it was not trained on."
Q9. What optimizer did you use and why?
"Sir, I used AdamW — Adam with Decoupled Weight Decay. Standard Adam includes weight decay in the gradient update, which causes interference. AdamW separates them, providing cleaner regularization. Combined with Cosine Annealing learning rate schedule, it achieves better convergence and generalization."
Q10. What is the difference between your project and the Kaggle DFDC winner?
"Sir, the Kaggle DFDC 1st-place solution by Selim Seferbekov uses a single EfficientNet-B7 spatial-only classifier. It has no frequency analysis, no explainability, no web interface, and is vulnerable to video compression. My project adds four novel contributions: a parallel DCT frequency stream with radial azimuthal spectrum formulation, attention-gated bilinear fusion, Grad-CAM explainability, and a Streamlit forensic dashboard."
Q11. What is MTCNN and how does it work?
"Sir, MTCNN stands for Multi-task Cascaded Convolutional Network. It's a three-stage face detection pipeline: the first stage (P-Net) proposes candidate face regions quickly, the second stage (R-Net) refines candidates, and the third stage (O-Net) outputs the final face bounding boxes with 5 facial landmarks. I use it to crop faces with a 15% contextual margin to capture blending artifacts at face boundaries."
Q12. What is AUC-ROC and why do you use it?
"Sir, AUC-ROC stands for Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve. It measures the model's ability to discriminate between real and fake videos across all possible classification thresholds, making it threshold-independent. A score of 1.0 is perfect. I target AUC-ROC ≥ 0.968, which outperforms baseline models like MesoNet (0.752) and XceptionNet (0.924)."
Q13. What is Squeeze-and-Excitation in EfficientNet?
"Sir, Squeeze-and-Excitation is a channel attention mechanism inside each MBConv block. It 'squeezes' the feature map using global average pooling to get a channel descriptor, then 'excites' by passing it through two fully connected layers with sigmoid activation to produce per-channel weights. This allows the network to focus on the most discriminative channels and suppress less useful ones."
Q14. How does your system handle video (not just images)?
"Sir, the system extracts frames from the input video at 5 frames per second using OpenCV. For each frame, MTCNN detects and crops the face. Each face crop is analyzed by the dual-stream model independently. The final video verdict is computed as the weighted average of per-frame predictions, with higher weights for frames where the model shows higher confidence."
Q15. What is the Radial Azimuthal DCT Spectrum?
"Sir, after computing the 2D DCT of a face image, I compute the radial average spectrum A(r) = average of all |F(u,v)| at radius r = √(u²+v²) from the DC component. This compresses a 2D frequency map into a 1D curve that is rotationally invariant. Real faces show a smooth exponential decline. Fake faces show periodic bumps corresponding to the GAN's upsampling grid frequency."
Q16. What is depthwise separable convolution?
"Sir, a standard convolution applies one 3×3 filter across all input channels simultaneously. A depthwise separable convolution splits this into two steps: first a depthwise convolution applies one filter per input channel independently, then a pointwise 1×1 convolution combines channels. This achieves similar results with 8–9× fewer operations, making the frequency stream CNN faster and lighter."
Q17. What is the GitHub link for your project?
"Sir, the complete project code, documentation, presentation, synopsis, and weekly log are available at github.com/DevRanaKaila/DeepGuard-AI."
Q18. What is layer normalization and where do you use it?
"Sir, layer normalization normalizes activations across the feature dimension (not the batch dimension like batch normalization). I use it in the classification head between the first FC layer and the ReLU activation. It stabilizes training and reduces the need for careful learning rate tuning, especially useful when batch sizes are small."
Q19. How is your system useful in real life?
"Sir, the system has four practical applications: (1) Digital banking KYC verification — detecting fake video calls used to impersonate customers. (2) Social media moderation — automatically flagging manipulated political videos before they spread. (3) Legal/judicial evidence verification — providing Grad-CAM heatmaps as forensic evidence showing where the manipulation occurred. (4) Corporate security — protecting against executive impersonation for fraudulent fund transfers."
Q20. What is Cosine Annealing learning rate schedule?
"Sir, cosine annealing periodically decreases the learning rate following a cosine curve from a maximum value (1e-4) to a minimum (1e-6) over T_0=10 epochs, then restarts. This 'warm restart' helps the model escape local minima and explore the loss landscape more effectively, often resulting in better final accuracy than a fixed learning rate."
Q21. What is the difference between spatial and frequency domain?
"Sir, the spatial domain represents an image as pixel values at specific positions — what you see with your eyes. The frequency domain represents the same image as frequency components — how fast pixel values change across the image. Low frequencies represent slow changes (smooth backgrounds), high frequencies represent rapid changes (edges, textures). GAN artifacts appear as periodic patterns in the frequency domain that are invisible in the spatial domain."
Q22. Why do GAN generators create checkerboard artifacts?
"Sir, GAN generators use transposed convolutions (also called deconvolutions) for upsampling — to increase image resolution from small to large. Due to the kernel stride, certain output pixels receive contributions from multiple filter positions while others receive fewer. This uneven overlap creates a periodic grid pattern — visible as checkerboard artifacts in the frequency domain."
Q23. What is early stopping and why did you use it?
"Sir, early stopping monitors validation loss during training. If the validation loss does not improve for 'patience' consecutive epochs (I set patience=6), training is stopped and the best checkpoint is saved. This prevents overfitting — the model memorizing training data instead of generalizing to new videos."
Q24. What is mixed precision training?
"Sir, mixed precision training uses 16-bit floating point (FP16) for forward pass computations and 32-bit (FP32) for gradient updates. This halves memory usage and doubles computation speed on compatible NVIDIA GPUs, without sacrificing accuracy. I implemented it using PyTorch's torch.cuda.amp (Automatic Mixed Precision) module."
Q25. What would you improve if you had more time?
"Sir, three improvements: First, I would add audio-visual synchronization analysis using lip-motion correlation with the audio signal, since deepfakes often desynchronize lip movements. Second, I would apply model quantization using TensorRT to reduce inference time for mobile deployment. Third, I would extend the frequency stream to detect artifacts from diffusion-model generators like Stable Diffusion and Sora, since these leave different spectral signatures than GANs."
